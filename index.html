Case study Information architecture EMEA division, financial institution

Eight platforms. One client. One calm view.

A bank’s client data lived in eight platforms. Role: UX lead on a six-week validation sprint. The result: a tested, executive-funded Single Client View.

RoleUX Lead
Timeline6 weeks
TeamInnovation team, UI designer, SMEs
OutcomeFunded for development
Chapter 01: The mess

Knowing a client meant logging into eight systems.

Client data was scattered across eight platforms. Legal and operational details lived in different places, and answering a simple question often needed emails between departments. Some tasks took weeks.

The Innovation Team wanted to build a single view on top of the Data Hub and MDM. First they needed evidence that users wanted it and that it was technically feasible.

Chapter 02: Listening

Workshops first, screens later.

Hands-on workshops and in-depth interviews with users and SMEs came first, and their findings became personas. Every later decision came back to one question: what does this person need to do their job today?

PersonasBuilt from qualitative research, so the team could argue from user needs instead of opinions.
JourneyTouchpoints and pain points mapped across departments, then scoped into an MVP flow.
StoriesRequirements written as user stories that engineering and business could both sign off.
Chapter 03: Structure

The real design was the filtering logic.

The work defined how information was categorized, how multi-dataset search and filters behaved, and how granular permissions shaped what each role could see. Favorites and recent interactions cut repeat searches from the daily workflow.
User flow for Single Client View · click any area to zoom in, click again to zoom out, drag to move around
Chapter 04: Iterate cheap

Sketch, test, then wireframe.

Each sketch was tested with clients before it became a wireframe. That kept iteration fast and cheap, and it let engineering check feasibility before scope was locked.

Chapter 05: Make it real

A high-fidelity prototype and an 8-minute film for the executive room.

Chapter 06: Outcome

Executives saw the film and funded the build.

About Cecile

From contracts to kinetic sculpture to AI.

UX architect and senior experience designer in New York, turning complex, regulated systems into clear, compliant, intuitive products. The method: strict logic and regulation, balanced with business strategy and empathy for the people who use them.

Five rooms, one habit

Each career left a lesson that’s still in use every day.

01LawRead the fine print. Regulation isn’t an obstacle to design; it’s part of the material. That’s why compliance-heavy products don’t scare me.
