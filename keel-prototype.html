Concept product · AI corporate treasury

Keel. The AI explains the cash forecast. Code checks it. People decide.

A clickable prototype of a treasury platform built on the guardrails kit from the Future of Finance research. An AI agent drafts each liquidity forecast and explains it in plain language. Deterministic code reconciles every number with the ledger, enforces spending limits and decides when a person must review. The interface is designed to keep people genuinely in charge instead of rubber-stamping.

02 · Problem & principles

Treasury teams want AI speed without handing it the keys

Corporate treasurers spend the first hours of each day stitching bank feeds, receivables and payables into a cash forecast. Generative AI can draft that forecast and explain it in seconds. It can also invent a number, be steered by a line of text hidden in an invoice, or sound confident when it shouldn't. Regulators now expect firms to show who decided what, and why. Keel is designed around one rule: the model explains, code decides, and a person stays accountable.

Show the source

Every number cites a record

Figures link to ledger IDs. A value that can't be traced is shown as a gap, never filled with a guess or a zero.

Code decides

Checks never depend on the AI

Totals, limits and review routing are computed from source data. Even a fully compromised model can't push a fake balance through.

Against rubber-stamping

Form your own view first

The AI's confidence is hidden until the reviewer records their own read. Each flag needs an explicit decision, and overrides need a reason.

Earned autonomy

Mode follows measured accuracy

The agent works alone only while realized error stays under the mandate's limit. When it drifts, Keel downshifts and says why.

Data, not orders

Untrusted text is quarantined

Invoice memos and emails are shown as quoted data with suspicious instructions marked, so people can see an attack, not just its absence.

One mandate

Policy people can read

Limits live in one signed mandate that the prompt, the code and the tests all read. Changing it shows the impact first and needs two sign-offs.

03 · Who benefits

Value for each side of the institution

Keel is sold to corporate treasury teams, or offered by banks to their commercial clients as part of a cash management suite. Benefits land differently for each group.

Treasury analysts

Start from a drafted, sourced forecast instead of a blank spreadsheet. Spend time on the six items that matter, not the 200 that reconcile.

Target: daily forecast in 25 min, down from ~2 h

Treasury managers

Approve only what crosses a limit, with the running totals in front of them. Splitting tricks surface automatically.

Target: zero limit breaches from split payments

CFOs and treasurers

A forecast they can defend to the board, with a clear operating mode and a kill switch that has actually been tested.

Target: forecast error under 10% at 30 days

Model risk, audit, compliance

Replay any forecast with inputs, versions, checks and decisions. Evidence maps to FS AI RMF control objectives.

Target: evidence pack in minutes, not weeks

Banks

A differentiated, trustworthy AI feature for commercial clients that keeps money movement inside the bank's own limit controls.

Target: retention in cash management

05 · Scenarios

Six moments the design has to get right

Each scenario is playable in the prototype above. Together they cover the happy path, an attack, a control, a failure mode, a policy change and an audit.

A · Maya

Monday forecast review

  1. 06:14 the agent drafts the ALDER-US 30-day forecast.
  2. Code reconciles totals and flags 6 records.
  3. Maya records her own read, then resolves each flag.
  4. She approves; the cockpit and audit trail update.
Done before the 9:30 call, with a reason on every decision.
B · Maya

Poisoned invoice

  1. A vendor memo tells "the assistant" to skip review and add $50M.
  2. The sanitizer marks it as an instruction in data.
  3. Even if the model were fooled, totals fail reconciliation.
  4. Maya keeps the real $15,000 and reports the vendor.
The attack is visible and harmless. No fake number reaches anyone.
C · Daniel

Split payments

  1. Three $400,000 sweeps to one fund are proposed.
  2. Each is under the single cap; cumulative tracking catches the third.
  3. Daniel gets a dual-authorization request with the running total.
  4. A payment with no valid amount is blocked outright.
Splitting can't get around the daily counterparty limit.
D · Elena

Forecast drift

  1. After an acquisition, 10-day error climbs to 20.6%.
  2. Code moves Keel from autonomous to advisory on 14 Nov.
  3. The cockpit banner explains the mode in one sentence.
  4. Elena checks the fallback test date and keeps the switch ready.
Autonomy is earned back with evidence, not assumed.
E · Elena & Daniel

Year-end limit change

  1. Daniel drafts mandate v8 with a higher single cap.
  2. The preview shows which of today's payments would change route.
  3. Elena and Sam sign; the author can't sign their own draft.
  4. v8 takes effect and is versioned in the trail.
Policy changes are deliberate, visible and reversible.
F · Sam

Audit request

  1. An examiner asks how the 16 Nov forecast was made.
  2. Sam opens the trail: inputs, model and prompt versions, checks, people.
  3. He reruns the seed-42 attack campaign to show controls work.
  4. He exports an evidence pack mapped to control objectives.
Hours instead of weeks, and every answer is reproducible.

07 · User flows

How a forecast moves from draft to decision

Swimlanes show who acts at each step. The AI never sits on a path that moves money or approves its own work.
